I am a **Senior Gravitational-Wave Research Fellow** at the **University of Birmingham**, working at the interface of gravitational-wave astronomy, fundamental physics, and compact-object astrophysics.

My research focuses on using gravitational-wave observations to test **General Relativity**, infer the astrophysical properties of compact binaries, and understand the physics of neutron stars and black holes. I work across **gravitational-wave data analysis, waveform modelling, and tests of gravity**, with an interest in extracting fundamental physics from current and next-generation detectors.

I have been a member of the **LIGO Scientific Collaboration since 2017**. Since January 2025, I have co-chaired the **Fundamental Physics Working Group**, contributing to analyses that use gravitational-wave observations to probe the foundations of gravity.

I also lead the **Cosmic Explorer Science Traceability Studies** and serve as one of the three coordinators of the **Fundamental Physics Division of the Einstein Telescope Collaboration's Observational Science Board**.

My broader research interests include the science potential of next-generation and space-based gravitational-wave observatories, including **Cosmic Explorer (CE), Einstein Telescope (ET), LISA, and LGWA**.

## Research

My research interests include:

- Tests of General Relativity with gravitational waves
- Compact-object astrophysics
- Black holes and neutron stars
- Gravitational-wave data analysis
- Gravitational-waveform modelling
- Fundamental physics with gravitational-wave observations
- Science cases for next-generation gravitational-wave detectors
- Cosmic Explorer and Einstein Telescope
- Space-based and lunar gravitational-wave observatories

## Collaboration and service

I am actively involved in the gravitational-wave community through the LIGO Scientific Collaboration and the Einstein Telescope Collaboration, with particular interests in coordinating research that connects gravitational-wave observations with fundamental physics.
